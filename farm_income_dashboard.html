Key Research Insights
Evidence-based findings from model analysis and feature importance study.
57.2%
Land area contribution
Land area is the single most dominant predictor of farm household income, accounting for 57.2% of MDI feature importance. Every additional hectare adds approximately ₹25,000–₹40,000 in annual income depending on crop and soil quality.
+₹18K
Irrigation premium
Irrigated farms earn on average ₹18,000 more per year than unirrigated ones — equivalent to ~20% of median farm income. Drip and sprinkler irrigation show the highest premiums among irrigation types.
₹25K
Diversification gain
Moving from monoculture (CDI ≈ 0) to moderate diversification (CDI ≈ 0.6, 3+ crops) yields an average income boost of ₹15,000–₹25,000 per year, reducing weather and price risk simultaneously.
+20%
Good soil premium
Households farming on good-quality soil (index = 3) earn approximately 20% more than those on poor-quality land (index = 1), even when controlling for land area and irrigation access.
D9–D10
Tail prediction error
Model error is highest in the top income deciles (₹2.5L+), where prediction MAPE rises to ~15%. This is expected with right-skewed income distributions and reflects genuine variability in high-income households.
₹146K
Sugarcane top earner
Sugarcane growers have the highest average income (₹146K/yr), followed by Vegetables (₹141K) and Cotton (₹130K). Millets, Pulses, and Soybean trail at ₹90K–₹95K on average.
Policy Implications
💧 Irrigation Expansion
Extending irrigation coverage from 53% to 70% nationally could lift farm incomes by an estimated ₹9,000–₹15,000 per household. Drip irrigation yields the highest return per rupee invested.
🌱 Diversification Incentives
Government schemes promoting crop diversification (e.g., moving from single-crop to 3+ varieties) have a measurable income impact. MSP support for diverse crops would accelerate adoption.
📏 Land Reform
With 86% of households being small/marginal farmers (<2 ha), land consolidation schemes and cooperative farming models could significantly boost incomes for this vulnerable segment.
🧪 Soil Health Cards
The 20% income differential between good and poor soil quality underscores the value of soil health card schemes and targeted soil improvement subsidies for low-soil-quality regions.

About This Project
Academic machine learning project for Indian farm household income prediction using Gradient Boosting.
ML Pipeline
1
Data Generation
5,000 synthetic households generated using NABARD NAFIS and NSSO survey statistics. Log-normal land distribution to match 86% small-farmer reality.
2
Preprocessing
Label encoding for 4 categorical features (State, Primary Crop, Season, Irrigation Type). 80/20 train-test split with random_state=42.
3
Modelling
GradientBoostingRegressor (sklearn 1.8.0) with 300 trees, learning rate 0.08, max depth 5, subsample 0.85.
4
Evaluation
5-fold cross-validation. Metrics: R², RMSE, MAE, MAPE. Error analysis by income decile reveals higher error in top-10% earners.
5
Explainability
Custom SHAP-style waterfall plots using marginal ablation decomposition. Feature contributions sum exactly to (prediction − baseline).
Data Sources
  • NABARD NAFIS 2016–17 — National income & expenditure benchmarks for farm households
  • NSSO 77th Round — Situation Assessment Survey of Agricultural Households
  • MoSPI — Ministry of Statistics land & crop distribution data
  • DAC&FW — Department of Agriculture crop-wise productivity statistics
Limitations
This project uses a synthetic dataset — findings are directionally valid but not suited for direct policy decisions without real survey data. Income predictions are estimates based on learned patterns, not ground-truth measurements. Model accuracy is lower at high income levels (top deciles).
